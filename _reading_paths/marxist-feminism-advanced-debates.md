introduction: This advanced pathway extends social reproduction theory to the world scale.
  Investigating the intersection of gender oppression, imperialism, racialized labor regimes,
  and state restructuring, it interrogates how contemporary financialized capitalism offsets
  its domestic profitability crises by externalizing the costs of human care onto migrant
  workers and the colonized peripheries.
summary: By the end, you should be able to analyze how global capitalism uses housewifization
  and subsistence extraction in the Global South, track the transnational transfer of reproductive
  labor across global care chains, evaluate current value-form disputes regarding the boundaries
  of capitalist valorization, and explain Nancy Fraser's thesis of the structural crisis
  of care under neoliberalism.
outcomes:
- Analyze Maria Mies's concept of global housewifization and the imperialist expropriation of unwaged subsistence producers.
- Trace how global care chains transfer reproductive deficits from the imperialist core to racialized migrant women from the periphery.
- Evaluate theoretical disputes over whether reproductive activity constitutes an uncommodified "outside" or an endogenous circuit of capital accumulation.
- Analyze the contemporary systemic crisis of care as an inevitable contradiction between capitalist valorization and human social reproduction.

questions:
- How does Maria Mies challenge classical definitions of the working class by placing unwaged third-world women and subsistence farmers at the core of global capital accumulation?
- What are "global care chains," and how do they demonstrate that the social reproductive deficit of wealthier nations is solved through an imperialist transfer of labor from poorer nations?
- Why does Nancy Fraser characterize capitalism's relation to social reproduction as "cannibalistic," and how does this crisis manifest in contemporary healthcare, education, and eldercare?
- In what ways does expanding the concept of class struggle to encompass social reproduction (strikes for clean water, public nurseries, reproductive healthcare) alter revolutionary strategy?
glossary:
- term: Global Care Chains
  definition: A series of personal and economic links based on the paid or unpaid work of care across national borders, typically running from the Global South to the imperialist core.
- term: Subsistence Production
  definition: Production aimed directly at the creation and maintenance of immediate life and livelihoods, which capital relies upon as an externalized, uncompensated foundation.
- term: Care Deficit
  definition: The systemic shortage of time, resources, and institutional capacity available to care for children, the ill, and the elderly under intense economic pressure and state austerity.
- term: Crisis of Social Reproduction
  definition: A structural contradiction wherein capital's relentless drive to cheapen, commodify, and extract surplus value threatens to exhaust the biological and social capacities needed to regenerate labor power.
- term: Value-Form Theory
  definition: A strand of Marxist political economy that analyzes the specific social forms (commodity, value, money, capital) through which human labor is socially validated in capitalist society.
- term: Life-Making
  definition: Activities and processes directed toward the sustenance, development, and flourishing of human beings, contrasted with the capital-making imperative of valorization.

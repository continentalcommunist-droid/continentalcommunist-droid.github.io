introduction: This pathway investigates how capitalism maintains and regenerates its most
  essential commodity—the living laborer. Moving through the 1970s Domestic Labor Debate,
  the critique of dual-systems theories, and modern Social Reproduction Theory (SRT), this
  curriculum shows that the exploitation of waged labor in the workplace is inextricably
  tied to the gendered, unwaged reproduction of life in the household and community.
summary: By the end, you should be able to reconstruct the technical debates over whether
  domestic labor creates economic value, evaluate the theoretical differences between
  dual-systems and unitary frameworks, explain how primitive accumulation restructured the
  female body as an instrument of demographic reproduction, and use Social Reproduction
  Theory to analyze labor struggles in health, education, and care.
outcomes:
- Reconstruct the arguments of the 1970s Domestic Labor Debate regarding housework, value-form, and the value of labor power.
- Distinguish dual-systems theory from Lise Vogel's unitary social reproduction framework.
- Explain Silvia Federici's historical thesis on the enclosure of the commons, the witch hunts, and the subordination of women during primitive accumulation.
- Analyze how contemporary strikes by teachers, nurses, and reproductive laborers challenge capitalist accumulation.

lectures:
- id: domestic-labor-value
  title: 'The domestic labor debate: Does housework produce value?'
  duration: 45-minute lecture guide
  summary: Deconstructs the arguments between the Wages for Housework tendency and value-theory
    critics concerning whether unwaged domestic tasks generate surplus value or merely
    reproduce the commodity of labor power.
  outline:
  - Marx's distinction between productive and unproductive labor under capital
  - The Italian Autonomist argument: the community as a social factory
  - Seccombe, Gardiner, and the value of labor power
  - Political consequences: state compensation, domestic strikes, or socialization of care
- id: dual-systems-versus-unitary
  title: 'Dual systems versus unitary theory: Capitalism and patriarchy'
  duration: 45-minute lecture guide
  summary: Examines Heidi Hartmann's dual-systems synthesis and contrasts it with Lise
    Vogel's breakthrough unitary formulation of social reproduction.
  outline:
  - Hartmann's critique: why capitalism alone cannot explain male dominance
  - The limits of treating patriarchy and capitalism as autonomous mechanisms
  - Vogel's alternative: generational replacement, daily renewal, and biological differences
  - Overcoming the false dichotomy between class struggle and feminist liberation
- id: primitive-accumulation-body
  title: 'Primitive accumulation, witch hunts, and the enclosure of the body'
  duration: 50-minute lecture guide
  summary: Explores Silvia Federici's historical thesis that the transition to capitalism
    depended upon the violent expropriation of women's communal knowledge, autonomy,
    and reproductive power.
  outline:
  - Beyond enclosures of land: enclosures of the commons of care and reproduction
  - The demographic crisis of the 16th century and state control over reproduction
  - Witch hunts as systematic class terror and social division
  - The historical construction of the "housewife" as a subjugated, unwaged laborer
questions:
- What is the precise economic distinction between saying that housework is useful and necessary to society versus saying that housework produces surplus value under capitalist value relations?
- Why did Lise Vogel reject Heidi Hartmann's dual-systems framework, and how does her unitary theory explain gender oppression within the reproduction of labor power?
- According to Silvia Federici, why did the transition to capitalism require an assault on female bodily autonomy through the criminalization of abortion, infanticide, and traditional midwifery?
- How does contemporary Social Reproduction Theory explain why budget austerity and public service cuts disproportionately affect women and racialized communities?
glossary:
- term: Social Reproduction
  definition: The daily, generational, and communal processes required to maintain, replenish, and reproduce the working class and human life as a whole.
- term: Value of Labor Power
  definition: In Marxian economics, the quantity of socially necessary labor time required to produce the means of subsistence necessary to maintain and reproduce the worker and their dependents.
- term: Domestic Labor Debate
  definition: A theoretical controversy in the 1970s among socialist feminists over whether unpaid housework produces value and surplus value or belongs outside the direct valorization process.
- term: Dual-Systems Theory
  definition: The perspective formulated by Heidi Hartmann and others that capitalism and patriarchy are two distinct, autonomous systems of material oppression that intersect in modern society.
- term: Unitary Theory
  definition: The theoretical framework developed by Lise Vogel arguing that women's oppression is rooted within the contradictory dynamics of capitalist social reproduction itself, eliminating the need for two separate systems.
- term: Housewifization
  definition: The institutional and legal process by which women's labor was defined as natural, non-economic domestic care rather than recognized as productive economic activity.

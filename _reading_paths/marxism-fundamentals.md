introduction: This pathway is an orientation to Marxism as a method of inquiry rather
  than a collection of conclusions to memorize. It begins with short, accessible texts
  and moves from historical development and class conflict to wages, surplus value,
  political institutions, and socialist transition.
summary: By the end, you should be able to distinguish a materialist explanation from
  a purely moral or idealist one, explain the relationship between labor power and
  surplus value, and use class analysis without reducing every political event to
  a mechanical economic cause.
outcomes:
- Explain historical materialism in plain language without treating history as predetermined.
- Distinguish labor, labor power, value, wages, profit, and surplus value.
- Identify how class coalitions, institutions, and ideology shape political outcomes.
- Read later Marxist arguments with a working conceptual vocabulary.

  title: 'Socialism: Utopian and Scientific'
  scope: Read the complete text and note how Engels distinguishes moral schemes from

  guiding_questions:
  - What distinguishes utopian socialist schemes from the scientific materialist method developed by Marx and Engels?
  - How does Engels explain the contradiction between socialized production and private capitalist appropriation, and why does this generate periodic economic crises?
  url: "/reading-list/#friedrich-engels--socialism-utopian-and-scientific"
- id: wage-labour

  title: Wage Labour and Capital
  text: wage-labour-and-capital
  scope: Read the complete text; diagram the exchange between worker and employer.
  guiding_questions:
  - What is the difference between the worker selling their "labor" versus selling their "labor power" as a commodity?
  - How does Marx show that wages and profits stand in an inverse relation to one another?
  url: "/reading-list/#karl-marx--wage-labour-and-capital"
- id: value-price-profit
  author: Karl Marx
  title: Value, Price and Profit
  text: value-price-and-profit
  scope: Read the complete text, paying special attention to wages, profit, and trade-union
    struggle.
  guiding_questions:
  - Why was Citizen Weston's argument that wage increases merely cause price increases incorrect according to Marx?
  - How does Marx define surplus value, and why does the struggle over wages represent a fight over the division of the working day?
  url: "/reading-list/#karl-marx--value-price-and-profit"
- id: brumaire
  author: Karl Marx
  title: The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte
  scope: Read Parts I–III and VII as an example of materialist political analysis.
  guiding_questions:
  - How does Marx apply historical materialism to political events without reducing politics to a mechanical reflection of economics?
  - Why was the French peasantry susceptible to Bonapartist authoritarianism, and how did the executive state gain relative autonomy from conflicting class factions?
  url: "/reading-list/#karl-marx--the-eighteenth-brumaire-of-louis-bonaparte"
- id: gotha
  author: Karl Marx
  title: Critique of the Gotha Programme
  scope: Read the complete text; separate Marx's criticism of capitalism from his
    claims about transition.
  guiding_questions:
  - Why does Marx reject the Lassallean claim that "labor is the source of all wealth" and that society should provide an "undiminished proceeds of labor"?
  - What differentiates the lower phase of communist society from the higher phase ("from each according to their ability, to each according to their needs")?
  url: "/reading-list/#karl-marx--critique-of-the-gotha-programme"

questions:
- What makes a materialist explanation different from a moral condemnation of capitalism?
- Why does Marx distinguish labor power from labor, and what changes once that distinction
  is made?
- Where does The Eighteenth Brumaire complicate a simple two-class model of politics?
- Which claims in these texts are historical arguments, and which are political judgments
  that require separate evaluation?
glossary:
- term: Historical materialism
  definition: A method that explains social development through changing material
    conditions, productive forces, class relations, institutions, and political struggle.
- term: Class
  definition: A social position shaped by a group's relationship to production, property,
    labor, and control over surplus.
- term: Labor power
  definition: A worker's capacity to labor, sold for a wage under capitalism rather
    than the finished labor itself.
- term: Surplus value
  definition: The value produced beyond the value required to reproduce labor power
    and returned to capital through the production process.
- term: Ideology
  definition: Systems of ideas and practices through which social relations are interpreted,
    normalized, and contested.
- term: State
  definition: The institutions through which public authority, administration, law,
    and coercive power are organized.

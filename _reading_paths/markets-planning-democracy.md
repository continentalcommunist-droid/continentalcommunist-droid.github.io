prerequisites:
- Complete Capital and Political Economy or have equivalent grounding in value, firms,

introduction: Serious socialist analysis must explain how complex economies coordinate
  information, investment, production, innovation, and consumption. This pathway stages
  the strongest versions of the calculation debate and then moves beyond a binary
  choice between total planning and unregulated markets.
summary: By the end, you should be able to state the major information and incentive
  problems facing different coordination systems, compare several models of socialist
  planning, and evaluate institutional proposals by their ownership rules, decision
  rights, feedback mechanisms, and democratic accountability.
outcomes:
- Reconstruct the major arguments in the socialist calculation debate.
- Distinguish price signals, accounting, planning, bargaining, and administrative
  coordination.
- Explain how ownership, incentives, information, and political power interact.
- Evaluate a coordination proposal without relying on the label market or plan alone.

lectures:
- id: calculation-debate
  title: The socialist calculation debate
  duration: 45-minute lecture guide
  summary: A fair reconstruction of the Mises, Hayek, and Lange positions, including
    what each means by knowledge, price, ownership, and rational calculation.
  outline:
  - Calculation, scarcity, and accounting
  - Dispersed knowledge and price formation
  - Lange's trial-and-error response and its limits
- id: institutions-feedback
  title: Institutions, incentives, and feedback
  duration: 40-minute lecture guide
  summary: Why coordination depends on decision rights, feedback quality, budget constraints,
    and the ability to correct error.
  outline:
  - Hard and soft budget constraints
  - Principal-agent problems across ownership systems
  - Innovation, exit, voice, and organizational learning
- id: democratic-coordination
  title: Democratic coordination beyond the binary
  duration: 40-minute lecture guide
  summary: A design vocabulary for combining public planning, markets, negotiated
    coordination, workplace democracy, and universal provision.
  outline:
  - Match coordination mechanisms to the type of good and decision
  - Separate consumer markets, investment allocation, and essential services
  - Build transparency, participation, revision, and constitutional limits
questions:
- Which parts of the calculation problem concern ownership, and which concern information
  or organizational scale?
- Does Hayek's dispersed-knowledge argument imply private ownership, competitive markets,
  or only decentralized feedback?
- What institutional conditions produce a soft budget constraint, and can they appear
  in private firms as well as public ones?
- Design a mixed coordination system for housing or energy and explain where planning,
  prices, rules, and democratic voice belong.
glossary:
- term: Economic calculation
  definition: The comparison of alternative uses of scarce resources through some
    common accounting and decision framework.
- term: Dispersed knowledge
  definition: Context-specific information distributed across many people and organizations
    rather than available to a single decision maker.
- term: Price signal
  definition: Information communicated through changes in exchange prices, shaped
    by institutions, market structure, purchasing power, and rules.
- term: Soft budget constraint
  definition: A condition in which an organization expects losses to be covered, weakening
    the pressure to adjust behavior or exit.
- term: Market socialism
  definition: A family of systems combining social or public ownership with market
    exchange or market-like allocation mechanisms.
- term: Democratic planning
  definition: Collective coordination of major economic decisions through institutions
    that include participation, transparency, accountability, and revision.

checkpoint:
  item_id: cc.item.marxism-fundamentals.week-06.retrieval-01
  concept_id: cc.concept.socialist-transition
  min_length: 90
  prompt: "Close the source. Distinguish the lower and higher phases of communist society and explain why the difference is material rather than merely moral."
  model_answer: "In the lower phase, cooperative society emerges from capitalism with inherited scarcity, capacities, habits, and standards of equal right. Individual consumption may therefore be linked to contributed labor after common deductions, producing unequal results for people with unequal needs or abilities. A higher phase presupposes transformed production, social capacities, and the overcoming of a constraining division of labor, making distribution according to need materially possible rather than simply more generous in principle."
  feedback_points:
  - "Locates the lower phase in conditions inherited from capitalism."
  - "Explains how equal contribution rules can yield unequal individual results."
  - "Connects the higher phase to changed productive and social capacities."
  - "Avoids presenting the phases as a date-based or automatic sequence."
  source_pointer: "Revisit Marx's discussion of common deductions, equal right, and the conditions under which the narrow horizon of bourgeois right can be surpassed."
worked_example:
  claim: "A worker-owned plant can distribute every dollar of revenue equally among its members because no private owner takes profit."
  steps:
  - label: Start before distribution
    text: "The plant must replace materials and equipment and coordinate its production with energy, transport, and other social labor."
  - label: Identify common claims
    text: "Reserves, expansion, administration, care, education, disability support, and other shared needs require deductions from the total product."
  - label: Test the rule of equality
    text: "Equal payments can ignore differences in time, ability, dependents, health, and need."
  - label: Examine inherited conditions
    text: "Markets, debt, skills, technical capacity, and legal institutions do not disappear merely because ownership changes."
  - label: State the transition question
    text: "The cooperative matters, but the form and sufficiency of distribution depend on wider transformations in production and social provision."
faded_example:
  item_id: cc.item.marxism-fundamentals.week-06.faded-01
  estimated_minutes: 6
  min_length: 30
  claim: "A hypothetical farming federation ends private ownership of its land and allocates individual harvest shares by hours worked. Its council says adopting distribution by need next year will by itself complete the transition to communism."
  provided_steps:
  - label: Start from production and nature
    text: "The harvest depends on soil, water, seeds, equipment, and coordinated labor. Labor is essential, but natural conditions also supply material wealth and constrain what can be distributed."
  - label: Account for common provision
    text: "In this scenario, the federation first reserves seed, replaces equipment, and provides for shared services and people unable to work. Individual shares come from what remains."
  completion_steps:
  - id: test-equal-right
    label: Test the equal contribution rule
    prompt: "Why can equal harvest shares for equal hours still produce unequal outcomes for individuals?"
    model_answer: "People differ in health, ability to work, household size, and need. The same hours-based rule can therefore leave them with unequal means relative to their needs. Formal equality does not remove the inherited limits Marx discusses for the lower phase."
  - id: identify-material-conditions
    label: Identify conditions for a higher phase
    prompt: "What must change besides the council's distribution rule before provision according to need can be sustained?"
    model_answer: "Sustainable provision requires adequate production, shared skills and resources, and transformed social relations, including overcoming a constraining division of labor. Announcing a new rule or date does not create those capacities."
  - id: assess-transition
    label: Assess the transition claim
    prompt: "Give one concrete next step and one reason this federation's ownership change cannot alone establish a completed social transition."
    model_answer: "Workers and users could plan harvest reserves and common services together, testing needs against ecological and productive capacity. Collective land ownership changes control, but access to machinery, wider exchange relations, and political institutions still shape what the federation can achieve."
  feedback_points:
  - "Explains unequal needs or capacities under an equal hours-based rule."
  - "Connects provision by need to changed productive and social capacities."
  - "Includes nature or common provision when assessing available resources."
  - "Treats the phases as conditional social relations rather than a calendar or ownership label."
  source_pointer: "Revisit Part I of Critique of the Gotha Programme on nature and material wealth, deductions from the social product, equal right, and the conditions for a higher phase."
application:
  item_id: cc.item.marxism-fundamentals.week-06.application-01
  concept_id: cc.concept.distribution
  min_length: 120
  prompt: "A municipality proposes a publicly owned care network with universal access, elected worker-and-user councils, and guaranteed employment. Use at least three concepts from this pathway to assess both its transformative potential and its limits."
  model_answer: "The network could decommodify access and alter class power by replacing private control with accountable social provision. A materialist analysis would ask how care labor, facilities, funding, technology, and workforce capacity are organized; which coalitions can win and defend the change; and how municipal and higher-level state institutions constrain it. The wage relation may persist for workers, while distribution according to need advances within one sphere. Its transformative potential depends on whether collective control expands and links to wider changes in production, property, and political power rather than on the policy's label alone."
  feedback_points:
  - "Uses material conditions such as labor, funding, facilities, or productive capacity."
  - "Examines class coalitions, institutions, and political power."
  - "Distinguishes a change in distribution or access from a complete change in production relations."
  - "States both a real transformative possibility and a concrete limit."
  - "Integrates at least three pathway concepts rather than analyzing each in isolation."

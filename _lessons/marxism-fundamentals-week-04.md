checkpoint:
  item_id: cc.item.marxism-fundamentals.week-04.retrieval-01
  concept_id: cc.concept.surplus-value
  min_length: 70
  prompt: "Close the source. Explain surplus value using necessary and surplus labor time, and state why profit is not simply a surcharge added after production."
  model_answer: "Necessary labor time is the portion of the working day in which workers reproduce value equivalent to their labor power. During surplus labor time they continue producing value that is not returned to them as wages. Surplus value originates in that difference within production; profit is a transformed share of surplus value after costs, competition, and realization, not merely an arbitrary markup added to an otherwise complete value."
  feedback_points:
  - "Defines necessary labor time in relation to the value of labor power."
  - "Defines surplus labor time as value-producing work beyond that portion."
  - "Locates surplus value in production rather than treating profit as only a pricing decision."
  source_pointer: "Revisit Parts 7–11, especially the transition from labor power's value to the production of surplus value."
worked_example:
  claim: "A union's 8% wage increase must cause the firm's prices to rise by exactly 8%."
  steps:
  - label: Separate wages from total cost
    text: "Labor is only one component of cost, so an 8% wage increase is not an 8% increase in the cost of every unit."
  - label: Examine distribution
    text: "The wage increase can reduce the share of new value retained as profit rather than being fully passed into price."
  - label: Check market conditions
    text: "Competition, demand, productivity, contracts, and market power affect whether the firm can raise prices."
  - label: Track uneven effects
    text: "Changes in workers' spending can shift demand among sectors instead of producing one uniform price movement."
  - label: State the strategic limit
    text: "The wage gain matters, but recurring pressure over the division of the working day remains while the wage relation persists."

  min_length: 90
  prompt: "A factory installs equipment that raises output per hour by 20%, keeps prices roughly stable, and raises wages by 4%. Use the week's concepts to identify the questions workers should ask about the result."
  model_answer: "Workers should ask whether the new equipment changes the intensity or length of work, employment levels, and the value produced during the working day. They should compare the value needed to reproduce labor power with the larger output and examine how the resulting gains are divided among wages, depreciation, other costs, and profit. A 4% wage increase can be a real gain while the employer captures a larger surplus, so workers should assess both immediate living standards and their bargaining power over the labor process."
  feedback_points:

  - "Recognizes a wage gain without assuming it exhausts the question of exploitation."
  - "Connects immediate bargaining to the longer-term structure of the wage relation."

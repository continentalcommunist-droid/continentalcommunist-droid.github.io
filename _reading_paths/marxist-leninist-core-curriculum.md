prerequisites:
- Complete the Marxism Fundamentals pathway or be able to explain historical materialism, labor power, surplus value, class, and the state in your own words.
- Keep a study notebook that separates each author's claims, evidence, historical setting, and strategic conclusions.
- Read the assigned primary texts before consulting commentary or later doctrinal summaries.
introduction: >-
  This curriculum establishes a demanding primary-text core for the wider learning
  ecosystem. It begins with Marx and Engels on materialist method and capitalist
  production, then follows Lenin's development of state theory, revolutionary
  organization, concrete tactics, imperialism, national self-determination, and
  socialist transition. Stalin's later formulations appear at the end as historically
  influential systematizations to compare against the earlier texts, not as substitutes
  for reading them. The sequence is chronological where possible and repeatedly returns
  from doctrine to the concrete disputes and conditions in which each argument was made.
summary: >-
  By the end, you should be able to reconstruct the Marxist-Leninist framework as a
  connected argument: materialist method grounds the critique of political economy;
  class rule raises the problem of the state; revolutionary politics requires durable
  organization and concrete tactics; monopoly and finance capital reshape the world
  system; national and colonial questions alter revolutionary strategy; and socialist
  transition presents new political and economic contradictions. You should also be
  able to distinguish Marx's and Engels's arguments, Lenin's developments, and later
  Soviet codifications rather than collapsing them into a single timeless doctrine.
outcomes:
- Reconstruct the movement from historical materialism to the critique of capitalist production without substituting slogans for arguments.
- Explain Lenin's theory of the state and proletarian political power in relation to Marx's analysis of the Paris Commune.
- Distinguish class consciousness, political organization, party form, strategy, tactics, and mass initiative.
- Explain Lenin's account of monopoly, finance capital, imperialist rivalry, and war while identifying the historical evidence on which it relied.
- Relate national self-determination and colonial liberation to proletarian internationalism without treating every nationalism as politically identical.
- Compare the source texts with later Marxist-Leninist systematizations and identify both continuity and simplification.
stages:
- number: I
  title: Method, practice, and history
  weeks: Weeks 1–4
  focus: Establish the materialist method through Marx's break with contemplative materialism, the social production of consciousness, and Engels's retrospective account of that development.
  objectives:
  - Explain practice as a category of knowledge and social transformation.
  - Relate productive forces, relations of production, institutions, and consciousness without mechanical determinism.
  - Distinguish Marx's early formulations from Engels's later synthesis.
- number: II
  title: Capitalist production and accumulation
  weeks: Weeks 5–10
  focus: Move from wages and value to labor power, surplus value, machinery, accumulation, and the historical formation of capitalist property relations.
  objectives:
  - Trace the circuit from commodity exchange to capitalist production.
  - Explain absolute and relative surplus value.
  - Connect accumulation and technological change to class formation.
- number: III
  title: Class struggle, state, and revolution
  weeks: Weeks 11–14
  focus: Study Marx's concrete political histories and Lenin's reconstruction of the state problem through the experience of 1848, Bonapartism, and the Paris Commune.
  objectives:
  - Analyze coalitions and political forms within a class society.
  - Explain why Lenin distinguishes smashing the bourgeois state apparatus from simply taking office within it.
  - Compare dictatorship, democracy, transition, and the withering away of the state.
- number: IV
  title: Party, organization, and tactics
  weeks: Weeks 15–18
  focus: Follow the development from independent working-class organization to Lenin's arguments about political consciousness, revolutionary organization, alliances, and tactical flexibility.
  objectives:
  - Distinguish spontaneous struggle from socialist political consciousness.
  - Explain the relation among party, class, leaders, and masses.
  - Evaluate tactics in relation to concrete conditions rather than fixed formulas.
- number: V
  title: Imperialism and the national-colonial question
  weeks: Weeks 19–21
  focus: Examine monopoly capitalism, finance capital, world division, imperialist war, national self-determination, and the strategic place of colonial liberation.
  objectives:
  - Reconstruct Lenin's economic definition of imperialism.
  - Separate national equality, the right to secede, and support for a particular nationalist program.
  - Connect internationalism to struggles in oppressed and colonized nations.
- number: VI
  title: Socialist transition and systematization
  weeks: Weeks 22–24
  focus: Study early Soviet problems of administration, accounting, class transition, and economic organization before assessing later codifications of Leninism and dialectical materialism.
  objectives:
  - Identify contradictions Lenin expected to persist after a revolutionary transfer of power.
  - Distinguish emergency measures, transitional policies, and general theoretical claims.
  - Compare Stalin's systematizations with the Marx, Engels, and Lenin texts already completed.

lectures:
- id: praxis-history
  title: From praxis to the materialist conception of history
  duration: 50-minute lecture guide
  summary: Reconstructs the development from Marx's critique of contemplative materialism to a historical account of production, social relations, consciousness, and transformative practice.
  outline:
  - Feuerbach, materialism, and the active side developed by idealism
  - Real individuals, production, division of labor, and social consciousness
  - Base and superstructure as a research problem rather than a mechanical diagram
  - Engels's retrospective synthesis and the problem of later codification
- id: value-exploitation-accumulation
  title: Value, exploitation, and accumulation
  duration: 65-minute lecture guide
  summary: Connects the introductory wage texts to Capital's account of commodity value, labor power, surplus value, machinery, accumulation, and dispossession.
  outline:
  - Commodity form, abstract labor, value, and money
  - Labor power and the production of surplus value
  - Working time, machinery, cooperation, and class struggle
  - Accumulation, concentration, the reserve army, and primitive accumulation
- id: class-state-commune
  title: Class struggle, Bonapartism, and the Commune
  duration: 60-minute lecture guide
  summary: Uses Marx's political histories to explain why class rule takes multiple political forms and how the Commune reshaped the Marxist problem of the state.
  outline:
  - Class fractions, representation, alliances, and political crisis
  - Bonapartism and the relative autonomy of executive power
  - The institutional form of the Paris Commune
  - From Marx's accounts to Lenin's theory of smashing the state machine
- id: party-consciousness
  title: Political consciousness and revolutionary organization
  duration: 60-minute lecture guide
  summary: Situates What Is to Be Done? within the struggle against Economism and develops a precise account of the relations among class, movement, organization, and political theory.
  outline:
  - Economic struggle and political struggle
  - Spontaneity, consciousness, and the role of socialist theory
  - Organization under autocratic repression
  - Party, class, leaders, and masses across Lenin's later writings
- id: strategy-tactics
  title: Strategy, tactics, alliances, and concrete analysis
  duration: 55-minute lecture guide
  summary: Compares 1850, 1905, April 1917, and 1920 to show how Marxist-Leninist tactics change with class alignment, institutional conditions, and the development of struggle.
  outline:
  - Independent organization and temporary alliance
  - Democratic and socialist tasks in the 1905 framework
  - The April Theses and a changed revolutionary situation
  - Compromise, parliament, unions, and opposition to formulaic leftism
- id: monopoly-war
  title: Monopoly capitalism, imperialism, and war
  duration: 65-minute lecture guide
  summary: Reconstructs Lenin's synthesis of concentration, banking, finance capital, capital export, colonial division, unequal development, rivalry, and war.
  outline:
  - From free competition to monopoly
  - Banks, industrial capital, finance capital, and oligarchy
  - Export of capital and division of the world
  - Uneven development, rivalry, war, and the critique of ultra-imperialism
- id: nations-colonies
  title: Nations, colonies, and proletarian internationalism
  duration: 55-minute lecture guide
  summary: Clarifies Lenin's distinctions among national equality, the right to secede, concrete support for liberation movements, class independence, and international unity.
  outline:
  - The political content of self-determination
  - Oppressor nations and oppressed nations
  - National movements, class leadership, and conditional alliance
  - Colonial revolution and the strategic horizon of the Communist International
- id: transition-systematization
  title: Transition, administration, and the systematization of Leninism
  duration: 65-minute lecture guide
  summary: Examines the economic and administrative problems identified in early Soviet texts before comparing them with Stalin's later doctrinal syntheses.
  outline:
  - Political power and the persistence of mixed economic forms
  - Accounting, control, discipline, specialists, and mass participation
  - Foundations of Leninism as a post-revolutionary synthesis
  - What is gained and lost when a method becomes a fixed system of propositions
questions:
- How does Marx's concept of practice change the problem of whether a theory is true?
- Where do Marx and Engels describe determination, constraint, tendency, and agency differently, and why do those distinctions matter?
- How does the purchase of labor power make exploitation compatible with formally equal commodity exchange?
- What do Marx's histories of France reveal that cannot be derived from an abstract two-class model alone?
- Which claims in The State and Revolution depend on Marx's account of the Commune, and which are Lenin's own theoretical or strategic developments?
- How does the organizational argument of What Is to Be Done? change when read in the concrete conditions of tsarist illegality and the polemic against Economism?
- What changes between Two Tactics and the April Theses, and what does that comparison teach about concrete analysis?
- Which parts of Imperialism are definitions, which are historical evidence, and which are political conclusions?
- How does Lenin distinguish the right of national self-determination from political support for every nationalist movement?
- Where do Stalin's syntheses accurately organize the earlier texts, and where do they replace open historical analysis with fixed formulas?
glossary:
- term: Praxis
  definition: Conscious, purposive human activity through which people reproduce and transform social relations and test their understanding of the world.
- term: Dialectical materialism
  definition: A later name for the materialist approach that analyzes processes through development, contradiction, relation, and change rather than treating things as isolated and fixed.
- term: Historical materialism
  definition: An approach to social history centered on material production, class relations, institutions, struggle, and the historically specific forms of consciousness that arise within them.
- term: Mode of production
  definition: The historically specific unity of productive forces and relations of production through which a society organizes the production and reproduction of life.
- term: Labor power
  definition: The worker's capacity to labor, sold as a commodity under capitalism and capable of producing more value than is required for its own reproduction.
- term: Dictatorship of the proletariat
  definition: In the assigned texts, the transitional political rule of the working class after the displacement of bourgeois class power, not a synonym for one particular constitutional design.
- term: Class consciousness
  definition: An understanding of class position, common interests, political antagonisms, and historical tasks that develops through struggle, organization, theory, and experience.
- term: Vanguard party
  definition: A disciplined revolutionary organization that seeks to unite advanced workers, preserve strategic continuity, generalize experience, and contend for political leadership within the broader class movement.
- term: Democratic centralism
  definition: A principle combining internal deliberation and elected collective decision with unity in carrying out decisions; its institutional meaning has varied across parties and historical periods.
- term: Imperialism
  definition: In Lenin's synthesis, the monopoly stage of capitalism characterized by concentrated production, finance capital, capital export, international monopoly associations, and territorial division among major powers.
- term: National self-determination
  definition: The political right of a nation to determine its state relationship, including separation, which Lenin distinguishes from endorsing every nationalist program or abandoning class independence.
- term: Socialist transition
  definition: The contested period between capitalist class rule and a developed communist society in which inherited economic forms, classes, administrative practices, and international pressures continue to shape political struggle.

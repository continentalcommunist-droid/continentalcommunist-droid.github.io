  prompt: "Close the source. Explain the contradiction in M–C–M′ and how the purchase of labor power resolves it without abandoning exchange of equivalents."
  model_answer: "M–C–M′ begins and ends with money, so its purpose is a quantitative increase. Mere exchange of equivalents cannot create new aggregate value, while unequal exchange only redistributes existing value. The capitalist can nevertheless buy labor power at its value—the value required for its reproduction—and consume its use value in production. Because living labor can create more value during the working day than labor power costs, surplus arises in production through a commodity purchased in circulation."
  feedback_points:
  - "Contrasts circulation for use with circulation for expanded value."
  - "Explains why neither equal nor unequal exchange creates aggregate new value."
  - "Defines labor power's use value as the capacity to create value."
  - "Locates surplus in production while preserving the role of circulation."

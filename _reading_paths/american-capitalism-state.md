introduction: American capitalism developed through a specific constitutional order,
  continental expansion, public infrastructure, slavery, industrial corporations,
  and recurring transformations of state capacity. This pathway brings institutional
  history into conversation with political economy rather than treating the market
  and the state as separate worlds.
summary: By the end, you should be able to identify how public authority and private
  accumulation developed together, compare competing explanations of the American
  state, and analyze a policy institution in terms of its legal structure, economic
  constituency, administrative capacity, and historical origin.
outcomes:
- Explain why markets require legal, political, and infrastructural institutions.
- Compare ideological, economic-interest, and institutional accounts of constitutional
  development.
- Trace the rise of the managerial corporation and the administrative state.
- Analyze the New Deal as both a response to crisis and a reorganization of political
  economy.
readings:
- id: federalist
  author: Alexander Hamilton, James Madison, and John Jay
  title: The Federalist Papers
  scope: Read Nos. 10, 23, 39, 47, 51, 62, 70, and 78 on faction, federal power, representation,
    and institutional design.
  guiding_questions:
  - In Federalist No. 10, how does Madison define "faction," and what economic causes does he identify as the most common and durable source of factional conflict?
  - How did the constitutional structure (separation of powers, bicameralism, federalism) aim to protect creditor and property rights while securing energetic central authority?
  url: "/reading-list/#the-federalist-papers"
- id: tocqueville
  author: Alexis de Tocqueville
  title: Democracy in America
  scope: Read selected chapters on associations, local government, equality, individualism,
    and majority rule.
  guiding_questions:
  - How does Tocqueville explain the relationship between social equality, individualism, and the danger of democratic despotism?
  - What role do voluntary associations, local self-government, and civil institutions play in checking or facilitating centralized power in American society?
  url: "/reading-list/#alexis-de-tocqueville--democracy-in-america"
- id: second-founding
  author: Eric Foner
  title: The Second Founding
  scope: Read the complete work as a study of constitutional transformation after
    slavery.
  guiding_questions:
  - How did the Thirteenth, Fourteenth, and Fifteenth Amendments redefine the constitutional order and the relationship between federal power and individual citizenship?
  - How was the egalitarian potential of the Reconstruction Amendments constrained or rolled back by conservative Supreme Court jurisprudence and political counterrevolution?
  url: "/reading-list/#eric-foner--the-second-founding"
- id: visible-hand
  author: Alfred D. Chandler Jr.
  title: The Visible Hand
  scope: Read the Introduction, conclusion, and selected industry chapters on managerial
    coordination.
  guiding_questions:
  - Why did the modern managerial enterprise replace market coordination in American transportation, communication, and mass production industries?
  - How did the separation of ownership from professional management transform corporate governance and market power in the late 19th and early 20th centuries?
  url: "/reading-list/#alfred-d-chandler-jr--the-visible-hand"
- id: age-roosevelt
  author: Arthur M. Schlesinger Jr.
  title: The Age of Roosevelt
  scope: Read selected chapters on the crisis of the old order and the construction
    of New Deal institutions.
  guiding_questions:
  - How did the Great Depression undermine the institutional and ideological legitimacy of the unregulated business order?
  - What structural changes did the New Deal make to federal administrative, monetary, and labor apparatuses, and what political compromises limited its scope?
  url: "/reading-list/#arthur-m-schlesinger-jr--the-age-of-roosevelt"
- id: power-elite
  author: C. Wright Mills
  title: The Power Elite
  scope: Read Parts I and III, then test Mills's institutional map against the present.
  guiding_questions:
  - What three institutional domains (corporate, military, political) compose the "power elite" in Mills's postwar analysis?
  - How does Mills critique pluralist theories of American democracy that claim power is widely dispersed among competing interest groups?
  url: "/reading-list/#c-wright-mills--the-power-elite"

  title: The corporation and the visible hand
  duration: 35-minute lecture guide
  summary: How railroads, national markets, managerial hierarchies, and public infrastructure

  outline:
  - Coordination inside firms versus coordination through markets
  - Infrastructure and the geography of accumulation
  - Corporate power and public regulation
- id: new-deal-state
  title: Crisis and the New Deal state
  duration: 40-minute lecture guide
  summary: An institutional account of how economic collapse, organized constituencies,

  outline:
  - Crisis as a struggle over institutional alternatives
  - Labor, business, agriculture, and regional coalitions
  - Capacity, compromise, exclusion, and durability
questions:
- Which American institutions restrain concentrated power, and which can reproduce
  it?
- How does Chandler's account of planning inside firms complicate a simple market-versus-planning
  divide?
- Was the New Deal primarily a concession to labor, a stabilization of capitalism,
  a democratic reform, or some combination?
- Choose one present institution and map its constitutional authority, administrative
  capacity, and economic constituency.
glossary:
- term: Faction
  definition: In Federalist No. 10, a group united by interests or passions that may
    conflict with the rights of others or the public good.
- term: Federalism
  definition: A constitutional division of authority between a national government
    and subnational governments.
- term: State capacity
  definition: The practical ability of public institutions to gather information,
    raise resources, administer programs, and enforce decisions.
- term: Managerial capitalism
  definition: A form of capitalism in which large enterprises are coordinated through
    professional administrative hierarchies.
- term: Regulatory state
  definition: Public institutions that shape economic conduct through rules, oversight,
    adjudication, and standard setting.
- term: Power elite
  definition: Mills's term for interlocking leadership circles across major corporate,
    political, and military institutions.

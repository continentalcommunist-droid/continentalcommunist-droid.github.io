checkpoint:
  item_id: cc.item.marxism-fundamentals.week-05.retrieval-01
  concept_id: cc.concept.relative-autonomy
  min_length: 80
  prompt: "Close the source. Explain what it means for the state to have relative autonomy without claiming that it is neutral or independent of class society."
  model_answer: "Relative autonomy means that state officials and institutions can pursue their own routines, coalitions, and strategies and can act against the immediate preference of particular capitalist factions. Yet they operate within and help reproduce a social order structured by property, class power, taxation, administration, and coercion. Autonomy is therefore relational and limited: it explains mediation inside a class society, not political neutrality outside it."
  feedback_points:
  - "Allows state institutions or officials some independent capacity."
  - "Distinguishes the capitalist class as a whole from particular competing fractions."
  - "Explains how state action can still reproduce the wider class order."
  - "Avoids equating relative autonomy with neutrality."
  source_pointer: "Revisit Part VII's account of the executive power and compare it with the shifting alliances in Parts I–III."
worked_example:
  claim: "A city rejects a rezoning request from a major developer, proving the planning state is independent of capital."
  steps:
  - label: Disaggregate interests
    text: "Developers, incumbent property owners, employers, lenders, tenants, and construction firms may want different outcomes."
  - label: Identify institutions
    text: "Planning rules, courts, elected offices, administrative staff, and neighborhood hearings structure which claims can prevail."
  - label: Examine political representation
    text: "Organizations and officials translate social positions into coalitions; no group's preferences enter government automatically."
  - label: Test the broader reproduction
    text: "Rejecting one project may defend property values, political legitimacy, or a different development strategy within the same order."
  - label: State the limit
    text: "The decision shows institutional mediation, but one conflict cannot establish that the state is detached from class relations."

  prompt: "During an economic emergency, an executive suspends ordinary contracting rules. Large retailers support the move, small businesses oppose it, public workers are divided, and the policy remains popular among many wage earners. Build a materialist political explanation without reducing the outcome to 'business wanted it.'"
  model_answer: "The explanation should separate the interests of large retailers from smaller firms and identify how public workers and wage earners are positioned unevenly by the emergency. It should examine the executive's institutional powers, administrative goals, legal inheritance, public narrative, and electoral coalition. The policy may advance some capitals, discipline others, and preserve legitimacy or continuity for the wider order. Its popularity and institutional form are causal parts of the outcome, not disguises to discard."
  feedback_points:
  - "Distinguishes class fractions instead of treating business as a single actor."
  - "Maps a coalition that includes groups with mixed or contradictory interests."
  - "Includes executive powers, rules, ideology, or legitimacy as mediating causes."
  - "Explains the wider order the policy may reproduce while acknowledging conflict within it."

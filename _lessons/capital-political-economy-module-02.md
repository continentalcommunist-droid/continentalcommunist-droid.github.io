  model_answer: "Ricardo argues that relative exchange values depend mainly on the comparative quantities of labor required to produce commodities, including labor embodied in tools and materials, rather than on the wage paid for that labor. He then has to qualify the relation for differences in capital durability, ratios of fixed to circulating capital, turnover time, and changes in distribution between wages and profits. Scarce goods and land rent also require separate treatment."

worked_example:
  claim: "Country A needs fewer hours than Country B to make both cloth and wine, so trade cannot benefit Country A."
  steps:
  - label: Separate absolute from comparative cost
    text: "Absolute productivity asks who uses fewer hours; comparative cost asks what each country gives up to produce one good rather than the other."
  - label: Calculate opportunity ratios
    text: "Compare cloth in units of forgone wine within each country, not only hours across countries."
  - label: Identify a possible specialization
    text: "Different internal ratios can create room for exchange even when one country is absolutely more productive in both goods."
  - label: Add movement assumptions
    text: "Ricardo's international result assumes capital and labor are less mobile across borders than goods."
  - label: Separate aggregate gain from distribution
    text: "A favorable exchange ratio does not determine which workers, owners, regions, or states capture the benefit."

  prompt: "Food prices rise for a sustained period while money wages initially remain fixed. Use Ricardo's framework to explain the pressure this creates on wages and profits, then name one assumption you would test."
  model_answer: "If workers must obtain a customary subsistence bundle, sustained food-price increases put upward pressure on money wages. With output prices and productivity otherwise fixed, a larger wage share reduces the profit share, producing Ricardo's inverse movement between wages and profits. The analysis should test whether the consumption bundle changes, productivity rises, firms can change prices, rents absorb part of the change, or workers possess enough bargaining power to secure the required wage increase."
  feedback_points:

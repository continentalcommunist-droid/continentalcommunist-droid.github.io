checkpoint:
  item_id: cc.item.marxism-fundamentals.week-03.retrieval-01
  concept_id: cc.concept.labor-power
  min_length: 60
  prompt: "Close the source. Distinguish labor power from labor and explain why the distinction matters for understanding wages."
  model_answer: "Labor power is the worker's capacity to work, which is sold for a wage for a defined period. Labor is the activity performed after that capacity is put to use in production. The distinction matters because the wage can cover the socially conditioned cost of reproducing labor power while the worker's actual labor creates more value during the working day than the wage represents."
  feedback_points:
  - "Defines labor power as a capacity sold for a period."
  - "Defines labor as the activity performed in production."
  - "Explains that the wage need not equal all the value produced."
  source_pointer: "Revisit 'What Are Wages?' and 'Relation of Wage Labour to Capital,' focusing on the exchange before production and what follows inside it."
worked_example:
  claim: "A worker is paid $160 for an eight-hour shift, so the employer has paid for all eight hours of value created."
  steps:
  - label: Name what is purchased
    text: "The employer purchases the worker's capacity to labor for the shift, not a finished quantity of value known in advance."
  - label: Separate wage from output
    text: "The $160 wage is a cost of employing and reproducing labor power; it does not by itself measure the new value produced."
  - label: Examine the production process
    text: "The worker uses tools, materials, knowledge, and time to produce goods or services that the employer controls."
  - label: Compare value flows
    text: "If the shift adds more value than wages and other costs, the remainder can appear as profit after sale."
  - label: State the relation
    text: "The wage exchange can be contractually equal while preserving the worker's need to sell labor power and capital's control over production."
application:

  min_length: 80
  prompt: "A temp agency bills a warehouse $32 per worker-hour and pays workers $19 per hour. What would you need to analyze before calling the $13 difference profit or exploitation?"
  model_answer: "The billing difference is not automatically profit because the agency may pay payroll taxes, insurance, recruitment, administration, idle-time costs, and other expenses from it. An analysis should identify what the warehouse purchases from the agency, what workers sell, who controls the labor process, the costs of reproducing labor power, and how the value created in warehouse production is divided among the warehouse, agency, and workers. Exploitation concerns the social relation through which surplus labor is appropriated, not merely one visible price spread."
  feedback_points:
  - "Does not treat the full $13 spread as profit without examining costs."
  - "Distinguishes the agency contract from the worker's sale of labor power."
  - "Examines control of production and the division of value among multiple firms and workers."
  - "Defines exploitation as a relation involving surplus labor, not simply low pay or an unfair feeling."

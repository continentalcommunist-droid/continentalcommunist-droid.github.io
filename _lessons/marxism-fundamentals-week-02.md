checkpoint:
  item_id: cc.item.marxism-fundamentals.week-02.retrieval-01
  concept_id: cc.concept.utopian-socialism
  min_length: 60
  prompt: "Close the source. Explain the difference between utopian socialism and the materialist approach Engels calls scientific socialism."
  model_answer: "Utopian socialists criticize existing society by designing rational or just alternatives and appealing for their adoption. Engels's materialist approach instead asks how capitalism developed, what contradictions it reproduces, which classes and capacities it creates, and how struggle could transform those conditions. A desirable end still matters, but it is joined to a historical explanation of the forces and agents that could realize it."
  feedback_points:
  - "Describes utopian socialism as an ideal model or moral appeal."
  - "Describes the materialist approach as an analysis of historically developed relations and contradictions."
  - "Identifies classes, organization, or collective capacity as part of the path to change."
  source_pointer: "Revisit the transition from Engels's history of socialist ideas to his account of modern production and class conflict."
worked_example:
  claim: "A nonprofit housing blueprint will solve a city's rent crisis because its rules are fairer."
  steps:
  - label: Identify the desired arrangement
    text: "The proposal may cap rents, remove profit, and allocate homes by need. Those features describe an aim, not yet a transition."
  - label: Map the existing relations
    text: "Land ownership, credit, construction capacity, zoning, tenant income, and public authority shape what can be built and controlled."
  - label: Find the social agents
    text: "Tenants, construction workers, public agencies, lenders, and landlords have unequal interests and capacities to sustain or block the plan."
  - label: Locate the contradiction
    text: "Housing is produced through interdependent social labor, while access and surplus remain governed by private property and payment."
  - label: Turn the ideal into a strategy
    text: "A materialist proposal specifies how organization, resources, and political power can change those relations over time."
faded_example:
  item_id: cc.item.marxism-fundamentals.week-02.faded-01
  estimated_minutes: 6
  min_length: 30
  claim: "In a hypothetical region, campaigners propose a publicly controlled textile network. They say writing fair allocation rules is enough to make it succeed."
  provided_steps:
  - label: Identify the desired arrangement
    text: "The campaign wants clothing production coordinated around shared needs. That describes a goal, but does not yet explain how people could establish and sustain it."
  - label: Map the existing relations
    text: "In this scenario, many workers coordinate spinning, weaving, transport, and sales, while separate private firms own the equipment, arrange credit, and control the proceeds."
  completion_steps:
  - id: locate-contradiction
    label: Locate the contradiction
    prompt: "How does this textile network illustrate social production alongside private appropriation?"
    model_answer: "Clothing depends on coordinated work across many workplaces and services. Yet private firms control the assets and appropriate the results, so the social character of production does not itself give producers collective control."
  - id: identify-agents
    label: Identify agents and opposition
    prompt: "Which organized groups could carry the proposal forward, and whose power or interests could obstruct it?"
    model_answer: "Textile and transport workers, users, and public bodies could organize support and supply practical knowledge. Owners and lenders could resist losing control. The proposal needs evidence of the supporters' organization and capacity, not just their possible interest."
  - id: outline-transition
    label: Turn the aim into a strategy
    prompt: "Identify two concrete changes needed to move from fair rules to a functioning network."
    model_answer: "A strategy must explain how an organized coalition obtains control of equipment and financing, and how workers and users coordinate supplies and decisions. It must account for opposition and material resources as well as allocation rules."
  feedback_points:
  - "Distinguishes collective production from who controls its results."
  - "Names agents with practical capacities and a source of opposition."
  - "Explains a change in power or resources needed to realize the goal."
  source_pointer: "Revisit the final section of Socialism: Utopian and Scientific, tracing social production, capitalist appropriation, and the possibility of conscious social control."
application:
  item_id: cc.item.marxism-fundamentals.week-02.application-01
  concept_id: cc.concept.socialized-production
  min_length: 80
  prompt: "A group proposes turning a food-delivery platform into a worker cooperative. Apply Engels's distinction: what would make the proposal more than an attractive blueprint?"
  model_answer: "The proposal would need an account of the platform's existing ownership, financing, data, logistics, and dependence on restaurants and customers. It should identify the organized workers and allies able to obtain those assets, the conflicts they would face, and the institutions that could support the transition. It should also show how cooperative control addresses the contradiction between a socially coordinated delivery system and private appropriation, rather than assuming fair rules alone will sustain the change."
  feedback_points:
  - "Examines the platform's concrete ownership, financing, technology, or market relations."
  - "Identifies organized agents and likely opposition."
  - "Connects social coordination to the question of who controls and appropriates its results."
  - "Explains a plausible process of transition rather than only describing the desired endpoint."

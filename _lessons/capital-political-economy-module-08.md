  min_length: 120
  prompt: "Close the source. Distinguish the circuits of money capital, productive capital, and commodity capital and explain why none is a separate kind of economy."
  model_answer: "The money-capital circuit foregrounds an advance of money that purchases means of production and labor power and returns as expanded money. The productive-capital circuit foregrounds the renewal of production after output is sold. The commodity-capital circuit begins from commodity output containing transferred value and surplus that must be realized and replaced. These are different starting points or functional forms within one continuous movement; industrial capital must pass through all three while other capitals occupy the forms simultaneously."
  feedback_points:
  - "States what each circuit foregrounds rather than merely listing three formulas."
  - "Includes purchase, production, sale, and replacement."
  - "Treats money, productive, and commodity capital as functional forms in one movement."
  - "Explains why simultaneous continuity across different capitals matters."
  source_pointer: "Revisit Part I and ask what disappears from view when each circuit is treated as the exclusive form."

  min_length: 130
  prompt: "A semiconductor shortage halts automobile assembly while consumer demand for cars remains strong. Use circuits and reproduction to explain why demand alone cannot restore production."
  model_answer: "Automobile producers may possess money capital and face strong demand, but they cannot transform that money into the required means of production when chips are unavailable. Chip producers in turn depend on equipment, materials, skilled labor, energy, and long production lead times. The interruption leaves productive capacity idle, delays commodity completion and sale, ties up capital, and affects workers' consumption. Reproduction requires materially proportionate outputs and coordinated timing across departments; purchasing power cannot instantly create the missing use values or compress turnover time."

prerequisites:
- Complete Marxism Fundamentals or be comfortable with labor power, surplus value,
  and class.
- Basic familiarity with supply, demand, wages, profit, and interest is helpful but
  not required.
introduction: This pathway treats Capital as an investigation of a social system rather
  than a book of economic slogans. It places Marx in conversation with classical political
  economy and follows capital from the individual commodity through production, circulation,
  accumulation, competition, credit, and crisis.
summary: By the end, you should be able to reconstruct Marx's argument from commodity
  exchange to capital accumulation, explain why profit can appear to arise from exchange
  even when surplus originates in production, and identify the different roles of
  industrial, commercial, and financial capital.
outcomes:
- Follow the movement from commodity, value, and money to capital and wage labor.
- Explain absolute and relative surplus value and their relationship to technology.
- Distinguish production, circulation, realization, profit, interest, and rent.
- Use Marxian categories to formulate testable questions about a contemporary industry.

questions:
- Why does Marx begin with the commodity rather than with class struggle or the factory?
- How can rising productivity increase material wealth while intensifying pressures
  on workers and firms?
- What is gained by separating the production of surplus value from its realization
  and distribution?
- Choose one industry: which forms of capital, turnover constraints, and competitive
    pressures organize it?
glossary:
- term: Commodity
  definition: A good or service produced for exchange that has both a use value and
    a socially mediated exchange value.
- term: Value
  definition: The socially necessary labor time represented in commodities under generalized
    commodity production.
- term: Constant capital
  definition: Value advanced for means of production that is transferred to the output
    rather than creating new value.
- term: Variable capital
  definition: Value advanced for labor power, called variable because living labor
    can create value beyond its own reproduction cost.
- term: Accumulation
  definition: The conversion of surplus into additional capital, expanding the scale
    and changing the organization of production.
- term: Social reproduction
  definition: The recurring material and social processes through which production,
    households, labor power, and institutions are renewed.

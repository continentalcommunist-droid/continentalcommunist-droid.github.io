introduction: This pathway examines the historic socialist analysis of the woman question.
  Rather than treating gender subordination as an eternal biological fact or an exclusively
  cultural prejudice, classical Marxist feminism analyzes the family, division of labor,
  and sexual morality as institutions grounded in historical modes of production, property
  accumulation, and class rule.
summary: By the end, you should be able to explain how the rise of private property transformed
  kinship and the division of labor, distinguish proletarian feminism from liberal or bourgeois
  feminism, analyze how the socialization of domestic labor relates to emancipation, and
  apply Claudia Jones's framework of super-exploitation and triple oppression to working-class
  struggles.
outcomes:
- Explain Engels's thesis on the historical overthrow of mother-right and the emergence of the patriarchal monogamous family.
- Distinguish the class interests and political demands of working-class women from those of bourgeois suffragism.
- Identify the historical mechanisms by which household tasks and caregiving became individualized private responsibilities.
- Explain the intersection of racism, imperialism, and gender super-exploitation in the materialist analysis of Black women workers.
readings:
- id: engels-origin-family
  author: Friedrich Engels
  title: The Origin of the Family, Private Property and the State
  scope: Read Chapter II (The Family) and Chapter IX (Barbarism and Civilization), focusing
    on the shift from communal gens to private property and patriarchal monogamy.
  guiding_questions:
  - How does Engels connect the accumulation of private wealth in livestock and land to the historic overthrow of mother-right and the establishment of male paternal dominance?
  - Why does Engels argue that modern individual monogamy in capitalist society functions as a miniature economic unit founded on the open or concealed domestic servitude of women?
  url: "https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1884/origin-family/"
- id: eleanor-marx-woman-question
  author: Eleanor Marx and Edward Aveling
  title: 'The Woman Question: From a Socialist Point of View'
  scope: Read the complete 1886 essay analyzing sex inequality through the lens of political
    economy.
  guiding_questions:
  - Why do Marx and Aveling reject the idea that legal reforms alone can liberate women without overthrowing the capitalist property relations that produce economic dependence?
  - How do the authors compare the structural subordination of wives under marriage laws to the economic condition of wage workers selling labor power?
  url: "https://www.marxists.org/archive/eleanor-marx/works/womanq.htm"
- id: zetkin-proletarian-woman
  author: Clara Zetkin
  title: Only in Conjunction With the Proletarian Woman Will Socialism Be Victorious
  scope: Read the complete speech delivered to the 1896 Gotha Congress of the Social
    Democratic Party of Germany.
  guiding_questions:
  - How does Zetkin distinguish the liberation struggles of upper-class, bourgeois, and proletarian women based on their respective material relations to capital?
  - Why does Zetkin emphasize that the working woman's fight is not a battle against working men, but a common struggle against the capitalist class?
  url: "https://www.marxists.org/archive/zetkin/1896/10/proletarian-woman.htm"
- id: kollontai-social-basis
  author: Alexandra Kollontai
  title: The Social Basis of the Woman Question
  scope: Read the Introduction and Part I on the struggle for political and civil rights.
  guiding_questions:
  - What material contradictions does Kollontai identify between the interests of feminist feminists ("bourgeois equal-righters") and women of the working class?
  - Why does Kollontai argue that formal legal equality in an exploitative society cannot resolve the double burden of waged employment and domestic work?
  url: "https://www.marxists.org/archive/kollontai/1909/social-basis.htm"
- id: kollontai-communism-family
  author: Alexandra Kollontai
  title: Communism and the Family
  scope: Read the complete 1920 text outlining the reorganization of social reproduction
    and daily life under Soviet power.
  guiding_questions:
  - How does Kollontai envision the transformation of the household from a private economic consumption unit into socialized public services (communal dining, nurseries, laundries)?
  - What does Kollontai argue happens to interpersonal affection and marriage once economic compulsion, inheritance, and material dependence are removed?
  url: "https://www.marxists.org/archive/kollontai/1920/communism-family.htm"
- id: claudia-jones-negro-woman
  author: Claudia Jones
  title: An End to the Neglect of the Problems of the Negro Woman!
  scope: Read the complete 1949 political essay on the triple oppression of Black working
    women in the United States.
  guiding_questions:
  - How does Jones conceptualize the triple oppression of Black women as workers, as Black people, and as women within American imperialist capitalism?
  - Why does Jones argue that the industrial labor movement and white progressives will remain compromised as long as they fail to champion the demands of Black domestic and agricultural workers?
  url: "https://www.marxists.org/history/usa/parties/cpusa/1949/0600-jones-negrowoman.pdf"
lectures:
- id: kinship-property-family
  title: From matrilineal kinship to patriarchal property
  duration: 35-minute lecture guide
  summary: Reconstructs Engels's historical materialist account of how changes in productive
    techniques, surpluses, and private property overthrew egalitarian kinship structures
    and created the patriarchal household.
  outline:
  - The materialist premise of reproduction of life alongside production of subsistence
  - Domestication of animals, pastoral surplus, and the origin of private property
  - Paternity, inheritance, and the overthrow of mother-right
  - The monogamous bourgeois family as an economic unit of property transmission
- id: class-versus-bourgeois-feminism
  title: Proletarian feminism versus bourgeois suffragism
  duration: 40-minute lecture guide
  summary: Explores the debates led by Clara Zetkin and Alexandra Kollontai inside the
    Second International regarding why working women required independent organization
    rooted in the labor movement.
  outline:
  - The emergence of the bourgeois women's rights movement and its limits
  - Women's entry into factory wage labor as double burden and revolutionary potential
  - International Working Women's Day and the mobilization of working-class women
  - Universal suffrage as a tactical terrain rather than the ultimate horizon of emancipation
- id: socializing-reproduction-triple-oppression
  title: Socializing domestic labor and the triple oppression framework
  duration: 45-minute lecture guide
  summary: Connects Soviet experiments in socializing domestic care with Claudia Jones's
    mid-century American Marxist-Leninist analysis of Black women workers under racial
    capitalism.
  outline:
  - The private kitchen and nursery as fetters on human development
  - Early Soviet social policy: abortion rights, civil marriage, and communal facilities
  - Claudia Jones, Jim Crow, and the super-exploitation of Black domestic workers
  - Why genuine working-class solidarity requires organizing the most oppressed sectors of labor
questions:
- Why did Engels consider the defeat of matrilineal kinship "the world-historical defeat of the female sex," and how does this differ from liberal feminist accounts of patriarchy?
- How did Clara Zetkin and Alexandra Kollontai justify their political refusal to merge working women's groups into the broader bourgeois feminist movement?
- What economic differences exist between winning equal legal rights under capitalism and abolishing the domestic division of labor?
- In what ways does Claudia Jones's analysis of Black women anticipated and ground contemporary theories of intersectionality within a Marxist class framework?
glossary:
- term: Mother-right
  definition: A matrilineal system of kinship and inheritance prevalent in pre-state communal societies where descent and social affiliation were traced through the mother.
- term: Monogamous Family
  definition: The historic institutional family form that Engels argues emerged with private property to ensure undisputed paternity for the inheritance of wealth.
- term: Proletarian Feminism
  definition: A socialist political tradition that locates the struggle for women's liberation within the broader abolition of private capital and wage exploitation.
- term: Socialization of Domestic Labor
  definition: The transfer of cooking, cleaning, child care, and elderly care from isolated private households into public, collective social institutions.
- term: Triple Oppression
  definition: A framework developed by Claudia Jones analyzing how Black working women are simultaneously super-exploited by class, oppressed by race, and subjugated by gender.
- term: Super-exploitation
  definition: The extraction of surplus value where wages fall persistently below the value of labor power, characteristic of colonized and racially segmented labor forces.

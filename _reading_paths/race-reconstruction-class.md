prerequisites:
- Know the basic chronology of slavery, the Civil War, Reconstruction, and Jim Crow.
- Marxism Fundamentals or equivalent familiarity with class analysis is recommended.
introduction: American class formation cannot be understood apart from slavery, emancipation,
  land, citizenship, migration, and racial hierarchy. This pathway places Du Bois's
  account of the Civil War and Reconstruction at its center, then tests and extends
  it through constitutional, political, and social analysis.
summary: By the end, you should be able to analyze race as historically produced without
  treating it as unreal, explain Reconstruction as a conflict over labor and democratic
  power, and connect legal status, political institutions, property, and everyday
  practices in the reproduction of hierarchy.
outcomes:
- Explain the relationship between slavery, capitalism, war, and emancipation in competing
  historical accounts.
- Describe Reconstruction as a struggle over labor, land, citizenship, education,
  and state power.
- Distinguish racial categories from the institutions and practices that reproduce
  racial inequality.
- Evaluate how class solidarity is built, fractured, and reorganized under racial
  hierarchy.

lectures:
- id: slavery-general-strike
  title: Slavery, capital, and the general strike
  duration: 40-minute lecture guide
  summary: A reconstruction of Du Bois's argument about enslaved labor, wartime withdrawal,
    emancipation, and the remaking of state power.
  outline:
  - Slavery as property relation and labor system
  - Self-emancipation and military conflict
  - The meaning and limits of the general-strike thesis
- id: reconstruction-counterrevolution
  title: Reconstruction and counterrevolution
  duration: 40-minute lecture guide
  summary: Why formal emancipation opened struggles over land, schools, voting, public
    goods, and labor coercion—and why those struggles were defeated.
  outline:
  - The Freedmen's Bureau, constitutional change, and state capacity
  - Multiracial democracy and its social base
  - Violence, political compromise, and the restoration of planter power
- id: racecraft-material-relations
  title: Racecraft and material social relations
  duration: 35-minute lecture guide
  summary: A method for explaining racial categories through repeated practices and

  outline:
  - Racism as action and race as retrospective explanation
  - Law, housing, labor markets, and political institutions
  - Avoiding both biological essentialism and empty class reductionism
questions:
- What does Du Bois gain by interpreting the Civil War through labor rather than military
  leadership alone?
- Which institutions made Reconstruction's democratic possibilities material, and
  which weaknesses made them vulnerable?
- How does the concept of racecraft change the evidence we would gather to explain
  racial inequality?
- When can class-based political appeals overcome racial division, and when can they
  reproduce it?
glossary:
- term: Racial capitalism
  definition: A framework emphasizing that capitalist development has historically
    organized accumulation through differentiated and racialized labor, property,
    and political status.
- term: General strike of the enslaved
  definition: Du Bois's interpretation of enslaved people withdrawing labor and joining
    Union lines as a decisive force in destroying slavery.
- term: Reconstruction
  definition: The post-Civil War struggle to rebuild the Union and redefine freedom,
    citizenship, labor, land, and public authority.
- term: Public wage
  definition: Du Bois's term for the material benefits white workers could receive
    through preferential access to public institutions and services.
- term: Psychological wage
  definition: Social status and symbolic compensation attached to whiteness that could
    divide workers despite shared economic interests.
- term: Racecraft
  definition: Practices that produce racial categories and then make those categories
    appear to be the natural cause of the practices.

checkpoint:
  item_id: cc.item.marxism-fundamentals.week-06.retrieval-01
  concept_id: cc.concept.socialist-transition
  min_length: 90
  prompt: "Close the source. Distinguish the lower and higher phases of communist society and explain why the difference is material rather than merely moral."
  model_answer: "In the lower phase, cooperative society emerges from capitalism with inherited scarcity, capacities, habits, and standards of equal right. Individual consumption may therefore be linked to contributed labor after common deductions, producing unequal results for people with unequal needs or abilities. A higher phase presupposes transformed production, social capacities, and the overcoming of a constraining division of labor, making distribution according to need materially possible rather than simply more generous in principle."
  feedback_points:
  - "Locates the lower phase in conditions inherited from capitalism."
  - "Explains how equal contribution rules can yield unequal individual results."
  - "Connects the higher phase to changed productive and social capacities."
  - "Avoids presenting the phases as a date-based or automatic sequence."
  source_pointer: "Revisit Marx's discussion of common deductions, equal right, and the conditions under which the narrow horizon of bourgeois right can be surpassed."
worked_example:
  claim: "A worker-owned plant can distribute every dollar of revenue equally among its members because no private owner takes profit."
  steps:
  - label: Start before distribution
    text: "The plant must replace materials and equipment and coordinate its production with energy, transport, and other social labor."
  - label: Identify common claims
    text: "Reserves, expansion, administration, care, education, disability support, and other shared needs require deductions from the total product."
  - label: Test the rule of equality
    text: "Equal payments can ignore differences in time, ability, dependents, health, and need."
  - label: Examine inherited conditions
    text: "Markets, debt, skills, technical capacity, and legal institutions do not disappear merely because ownership changes."
  - label: State the transition question
    text: "The cooperative matters, but the form and sufficiency of distribution depend on wider transformations in production and social provision."

  model_answer: "The network could decommodify access and alter class power by replacing private control with accountable social provision. A materialist analysis would ask how care labor, facilities, funding, technology, and workforce capacity are organized; which coalitions can win and defend the change; and how municipal and higher-level state institutions constrain it. The wage relation may persist for workers, while distribution according to need advances within one sphere. Its transformative potential depends on whether collective control expands and links to wider changes in production, property, and political power rather than on the policy's label alone."

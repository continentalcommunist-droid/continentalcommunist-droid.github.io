  prompt: "Close the source. Distinguish use value, exchange value, and value, then explain why socially necessary—not individual—labor time matters."
  model_answer: "Use value names a commodity's capacity to satisfy a need and depends on its material qualities. Exchange value is the changing quantitative ratio in which it exchanges for other commodities. Value is the social substance expressed through those ratios: abstract human labor measured by the labor time socially necessary under normal conditions and average skill and intensity. Extra hours caused by an inefficient producer do not automatically add value because private labor is validated socially through exchange."
  feedback_points:
  - "Defines use value through usefulness or material qualities."
  - "Defines exchange value as a quantitative exchange relation or form of appearance."
  - "Connects value to abstract labor and socially necessary labor time."
  - "Explains why inefficient individual labor does not set value by itself."
  source_pointer: "Revisit Sections 1–2 and track Marx's movement from heterogeneous useful labors to abstract social labor."
worked_example:
  claim: "A hand-built table took twenty hours, while comparable tables normally take five, so it has four times the value."
  steps:
  - label: Confirm use value
    text: "The table must first be useful to someone; labor spent on an unusable object is not validated as commodity-producing labor."
  - label: Find normal conditions
    text: "Identify prevailing tools, methods, skill, and intensity for producing comparable tables."
  - label: Estimate socially necessary time
    text: "The social measure is closer to the five-hour norm than the maker's private twenty-hour expenditure."
  - label: Observe market validation
    text: "Exchange relates this private labor to the total social division of labor through prices and sale."
  - label: Preserve the distinction
    text: "A buyer may still pay more for craft qualities, scarcity, or reputation; that concrete price does not erase the analytical distinction."
application:

  concept_id: cc.concept.commodity-fetishism
  min_length: 120
  prompt: "A cloud-storage plan appears to buyers as a monthly quantity of gigabytes with a price. Explain what commodity fetishism would direct an analyst to recover behind that appearance."
  model_answer: "The plan appears as a property of a purchasable thing—so many gigabytes for so many dollars—while relations among people take the form of relations among services and prices. An analyst would recover the labor of data-center, maintenance, energy, software, logistics, and content-moderation workers; ownership of infrastructure and intellectual property; contracts and standards; and the social allocation of energy and computing capacity. The price relation is real and organizes behavior, but it obscures the cooperative and antagonistic relations through which the service exists."
  feedback_points:

  - "Recovers concrete labor and cooperation hidden by the purchasable service."
  - "Includes ownership, contracts, standards, or other relations of control."
  - "Acknowledges that the price form is socially effective rather than an illusion that can be ignored."

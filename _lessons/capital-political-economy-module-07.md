checkpoint:
  item_id: cc.item.capital-political-economy.week-07.retrieval-01
  concept_id: cc.concept.relative-surplus-population
  min_length: 120
  prompt: "Close the source. Explain why the relative surplus population is an internally produced condition of accumulation rather than simply a group left outside capitalism."
  model_answer: "Accumulation expands production and can increase employment, but competition also drives changes in productivity and capital composition that reduce labor required relative to the scale of output. Workers are displaced, drawn in, and expelled unevenly across firms, sectors, regions, and the business cycle. This available labor population disciplines wages and lets capital expand when profitable. It is therefore relative to capital's changing demand and continually reproduced by accumulation, not a fixed population wholly external to capitalist production."
  feedback_points:
  - "Allows accumulation to create jobs as well as displace labor."
  - "Connects productivity or capital composition to reduced labor needs relative to output."
  - "Describes movement across sectors, regions, or phases of the cycle."
  - "Explains the surplus population's role in wage discipline or rapid expansion."
  source_pointer: "Revisit Chapter 25's movement from enlarged capital to changing composition and the different forms of the relative surplus population."
worked_example:
  claim: "A logistics region added 8,000 jobs, so automation cannot be producing a surplus labor population there."
  steps:
  - label: Separate levels and rates
    text: "Total employment can grow while labor required per package or per dollar of output falls."
  - label: Map uneven movement
    text: "New hubs may hire as older facilities shed workers, subcontracting expands, or jobs migrate within the region."
  - label: Identify available labor
    text: "Applicants, temporary workers, part-time workers seeking hours, and displaced workers can form different surplus-population layers."
  - label: Examine discipline
    text: "A ready labor supply can shape wages, schedules, turnover policies, and workers' willingness to resist."
  - label: Specify evidence
    text: "Compare employment, output, hours, job tenure, applications, wages, and displacement over time rather than rely on one net number."

  provided_steps:
  - label: "Trace access to tools"
    text: "The town transfers its shared workshops and tools to the investor. Two hundred craftspeople lose their former access to independent production."

  completion_steps:
  - id: explain-social-separation
    label: "Explain how workers become available"
    prompt: "What does a story about saving miss about the formation of this workforce?"
    model_answer: "The transfer separates producers from tools and independent subsistence, making the sale of labor power necessary. The relevant process is the production of a property and labor relation, not simply the accumulation of money by a thrifty owner."
  - id: connect-jobs-and-surplus
    label: "Connect hiring to surplus population"
    prompt: "Can sixty new factory jobs coexist with a relative surplus population in this case?"
    model_answer: "Yes. Capital absorbs sixty workers while 140 of the displaced craftspeople remain available for work. The surplus is relative to capital's current demand, so expanding factory employment does not establish that everyone separated from production is absorbed."
  - id: test-mechanism-and-period
    label: "Test the explanation"
    prompt: "What evidence would distinguish this mechanism from a temporary demand downturn or a voluntary move?"
    model_answer: "Examine the transfer terms, prior tool-access rights, ability to continue independent production, job-seeking records, and timing of displacement. Compare demand and hiring before and after the transfer, and investigate resistance or alternative livelihoods rather than infer the mechanism from unemployment alone."
  feedback_points:
  - "Connects the change in property access to dependence on wage work."
  - "Allows capital to hire workers while a larger available workforce remains."
  - "Names evidence about separation and timing, not only a head count of unemployed people."

application:
  item_id: cc.item.capital-political-economy.week-07.application-01
  concept_id: cc.concept.primitive-accumulation
  min_length: 130
  prompt: "A rural region shifts from customary communal land use to titled parcels acquired by agribusiness, while former users enter seasonal wage work. Analyze this as a possible process of primitive accumulation without assuming the label settles the case."
  model_answer: "The analysis should determine how legal title, debt, taxation, policing, ecological change, or direct force separated users from effective access to land and subsistence. It should trace how land and resources became assets for accumulation while displaced people became available for wage labor. It must also identify resistance, compensation, hybrid livelihoods, and prior market relations. The key question is whether institutions are producing or deepening the separation between producers and means of production, not whether the change merely happened early in history."
  feedback_points:
  - "Examines concrete mechanisms of separation such as law, debt, taxation, or force."
  - "Connects asset formation for capital with the production of wage-dependent workers."
  - "Treats primitive accumulation as a social relation rather than a synonym for old theft or saving."
  - "Looks for resistance, mixed forms, and counterevidence."

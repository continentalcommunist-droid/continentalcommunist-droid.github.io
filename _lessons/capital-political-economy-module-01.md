checkpoint:
  item_id: cc.item.capital-political-economy.week-01.retrieval-01
  concept_id: cc.concept.division-of-labor
  min_length: 90
  prompt: "Close the source. Explain three ways the division of labor raises productivity, then identify one social condition that Smith's pin-factory example leaves outside the workshop."
  model_answer: "Smith links greater productivity to increased dexterity at a repeated task, time saved when workers no longer switch tasks, and the invention or use of task-specific machinery. The workshop example abstracts from conditions that make specialization possible, including the extent of the market, ownership of tools and output, coordination by employers, transport, education, and workers' dependence on wages. The technical gains therefore do not by themselves explain who controls or receives them."
  feedback_points:
  - "Names dexterity, reduced switching time, and machinery or invention."
  - "Connects specialization to the extent of exchange or the market."
  - "Identifies ownership, coordination, wage dependence, infrastructure, or another social condition outside the workshop."
  - "Does not infer the distribution of gains from productivity alone."
  source_pointer: "Revisit Chapters 1–3, then compare the pin factory with Smith's later discussion of wages and bargaining."
worked_example:
  claim: "A warehouse doubled output after dividing one job into six specialized tasks, so every participant must be better off."
  steps:
  - label: Identify the productivity mechanism
    text: "Repetition, less movement between tasks, and task-specific equipment can raise units handled per hour."
  - label: Map new dependencies
    text: "Workers now depend more heavily on coordination, shared machinery, logistics, and the sale of output."
  - label: Separate output from distribution
    text: "More goods do not determine how revenue is divided among wages, profit, rent, and reinvestment."
  - label: Examine bargaining positions
    text: "Replaceability, unemployment, legal rules, and workers' ability to organize affect who captures the gain."
  - label: State the conditional conclusion
    text: "Specialization can enlarge social wealth while its benefits and burdens remain institutionally contested."
application:
  item_id: cc.item.capital-political-economy.week-01.application-01
  concept_id: cc.concept.exchange-value
  min_length: 110
  prompt: "A coffee chain introduces a drink that customers value highly, takes little added time to prepare, and sells for twice the price of a basic coffee. Use Smith's distinctions to explain what the price does—and does not—tell you."
  model_answer: "The drink's usefulness to customers is its use value, while its money price expresses an exchange relation. Its higher market price may reflect demand, scarcity, branding, location, competitive conditions, and deviations from a longer-run center of price. The small added preparation time does not exhaust the labor and costs embodied across ingredients, equipment, rent, management, and distribution. Price reveals what buyers pay under current conditions; by itself it does not measure subjective usefulness, total labor, bargaining power, or how the revenue is divided."
  feedback_points:
  - "Distinguishes usefulness from the commodity's exchange relation or price."
  - "Treats market price as contingent on demand, scarcity, competition, or institutional conditions."
  - "Looks beyond the final preparation task to the wider production and distribution chain."
  - "Avoids treating price as a complete measure of usefulness, labor, or fairness."
  source_pointer: "Return to Chapters 4–7 and compare value in use, value in exchange, market price, and natural price."

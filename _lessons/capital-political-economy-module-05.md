checkpoint:

  concept_id: cc.concept.variable-capital
  min_length: 110
  prompt: "Close the source. Distinguish constant from variable capital and explain why the rate of surplus value is s/v rather than s divided by total capital."
  model_answer: "Constant capital is value advanced for means of production; its existing value is transferred to the product as materials and equipment are consumed. Variable capital purchases labor power, whose use in production creates new value greater than its own value. The rate of surplus value compares surplus labor with necessary labor, represented as s/v, and therefore measures exploitation of labor power. Dividing surplus by c plus v instead yields a profit-rate form that relates surplus to the entire capital advanced and obscures its source."
  feedback_points:
  - "Explains value transfer from means of production rather than saying machinery creates no useful output."
  - "Identifies living labor as the source of new value in the framework."
  - "Interprets v as the value advanced for labor power and s as surplus value."
  - "Distinguishes the exploitation measure s/v from a profit-rate denominator."
  source_pointer: "Revisit Chapters 8–9 and reproduce one of Marx's numerical divisions of the working day before reviewing the formulas."
worked_example:
  claim: "In a ten-hour shift, five hours reproduce the daily value of labor power; the exploitation rate is therefore 50%."
  steps:
  - label: Divide the working day
    text: "Five hours are necessary labor, leaving five hours as surplus labor."
  - label: Form the correct ratio
    text: "The rate of surplus value is surplus labor divided by necessary labor: 5/5."
  - label: Calculate the result
    text: "The rate is 100%, not the surplus portion's 50% share of the total day."
  - label: Avoid a price shortcut
    text: "The calculation requires a value relation; observed wages, sales, and accounting profit do not reveal it directly."
  - label: Identify the conflict
    text: "Extending the shift to twelve hours while necessary labor remains five raises surplus labor to seven and the rate to 140%."

  prompt: "A distribution center adds mandatory overtime during peak season while keeping hourly productivity approximately constant. Analyze the change and identify what evidence would show its limits."
  model_answer: "With productivity and the value of labor power provisionally fixed, longer hours extend surplus labor after necessary labor has been performed, a route to absolute surplus value. The analysis should measure paid hours, overtime premiums, output, injuries, absenteeism, turnover, and whether more labor is needed later to reproduce workers' capacity. Legal limits, worker resistance, hiring alternatives, demand, and physical exhaustion constrain extension. An overtime premium can raise wages while the longer day still increases the mass of surplus value."
